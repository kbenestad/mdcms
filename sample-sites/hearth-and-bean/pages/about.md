# About Hearth & Bean

I spent eight years in a lab before I spent a single day behind an espresso machine.

I'm Priya Sathe, and before Hearth & Bean I worked as an analytical chemist, mostly running HPLC and titration panels for a food-testing lab. It was careful, exacting work — measure twice, calibrate the instrument, don't trust a result you can't reproduce. I didn't expect any of it to be useful for making coffee. It turned out to be the most useful thing I know.

## How This Started

I got into coffee the way a lot of people in this industry did: badly, at home, with a bag of supermarket beans and a French press that made something bitter and thin no matter what I did. A friend gave me a bag of freshly roasted single-origin coffee and a scale for my birthday, mostly as a joke. I brewed it by weight instead of guessing, and it was such a different drink that I assumed I'd done something wrong. I hadn't. I'd just been measuring nothing, for years, and calling it coffee.

That set off the part of my brain that likes variables. Why does a finer grind extract faster? What is actually happening when coffee "blooms"? Why did the same beans taste different at two cafés three blocks apart? I started reading everything I could find — Illy's papers on extraction, the SCA water standard, roasting logs from small roasters willing to publish them — and within a year I had a home setup that would have embarrassed a small café: a refractometer, three brewers, a scale accurate to a tenth of a gram, and a spreadsheet I am not proud of.

## Why a Shop

I opened Hearth & Bean in 2019 because I wanted to roast coffee the way I used to run a lab panel: with real measurements, a repeatable process, and enough curiosity to keep asking why. Most of what makes coffee good or bad is invisible unless you go looking for it — extraction yield, total dissolved solids, water hardness, roast development time — and I wanted a place where we took that seriously without making the person waiting for their morning latte sit through a lecture. You can get a excellent, unremarkable cup here in ninety seconds. You can also ask a question that turns into a twenty-minute conversation. Both are the point.

We started as a two-person filter bar with a single sample roaster in the back corner. We now roast on a 12kg drum roaster three mornings a week, run six espresso-based drinks and a rotating filter list, and employ eleven people, several of whom know more about coffee than I do at this point and are better baristas besides.

## What We Believe In

**Traceability over marketing.** Every bag we sell lists the farm or cooperative, the varietal, the processing method, and the elevation, because that information changes how you should brew it and because the people who grew it deserve the credit.

**Freshness over convenience.** We roast in small batches and sell out of bags regularly rather than keeping a large buffer stock. Coffee is at its best 4–14 days after roasting; we would rather run out on a Tuesday than sell you something stale.

**Direct and long-term relationships.** We buy the large majority of our green coffee through direct trade relationships with importers who visit the farms every season, and we've bought from several of the same cooperatives for four years running. Continuity lets farmers plan, and it lets us actually taste how a lot changes year to year.

**Teaching, not gatekeeping.** Specialty coffee has a reputation for being precious about itself. We try hard not to be. If you want the science, we'll give you the science. If you just want a good flat white, we will never make you feel like you're doing coffee wrong.

## The Space

We're a narrow shop on Marlow Street with eighteen seats, a bar you can watch the espresso machine from, and a back corner where the roaster runs on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday mornings — if you're here early, you'll smell it before you see it. We're a genuinely good place to sit with a laptop, and an even better place to put the laptop away and talk to whoever's next to you.

Come say hello. Ask us what's on the filter bar today. We're glad you're here.

— Priya and the Hearth & Bean team
