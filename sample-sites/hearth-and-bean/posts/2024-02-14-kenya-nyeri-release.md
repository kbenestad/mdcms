New on the filter bar this week: a washed coffee from Nyeri County, Kenya, grown at around 1,750 metres by smallholder farmers delivering cherry to the Gakuyuini factory.

**In the cup:** blackcurrant, a bright, almost tomato-like acidity, and a long, syrupy finish. This is about as close as coffee gets to tasting like a specific fruit rather than "fruity" in the abstract.

**Why it tastes like this:** two things are doing the work. First, the SL28 and SL34 varietals grown widely in Kenya are genetically predisposed to intense, wine-like acidity — they were selected decades ago partly for drought resistance, but they carry this flavour signature as a side effect. Second, the washed process here is unusually rigorous: double fermentation (a dry ferment followed by a washed ferment) strips the mucilage more completely than a single-stage wash, which tends to produce an even cleaner, more transparent cup.

**How we're brewing it:** V60, 1:16, water just off the boil at 94°C. This coffee has enough acidity that a slightly coarser grind and shorter contact time keeps it bright without tipping sour. We're roasting it light — well before first crack development gets going — specifically to preserve that blackcurrant note; a darker roast would trade it for generic roast sweetness.

Available by the cup at the bar, or as a 250g bag to take home. Ask for it by name — "the Kenyan" — and we'll know what you mean for the next few weeks, until it sells out.
