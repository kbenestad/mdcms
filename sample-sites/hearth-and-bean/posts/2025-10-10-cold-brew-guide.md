People sometimes assume cold brew is just iced coffee, but it's a genuinely different extraction process, and it's worth understanding why the recipe looks so different from anything you'd use with hot water.

## The Recipe

- **Coffee:** coarse grind (coarser than you'd use for a French press), 1:8 ratio with room-temperature or cold filtered water
- **Steep:** 16–18 hours at room temperature, or slightly longer in the fridge
- **Filter:** through a paper filter or fine mesh, twice if the first pass leaves sediment
- **Serve:** the result is a concentrate — dilute roughly 1:1 with water or milk over ice to taste

## Why It's So Different From Hot Brewing

Extraction happens faster at higher temperatures because heat speeds up the rate at which soluble compounds dissolve out of the coffee grounds — the same reason tea steeps faster in boiling water than in cold water. Cold brew compensates for the much slower extraction rate by trading time for temperature: instead of 2–5 minutes at 90+°C, you get 16+ hours at room temperature or below.

That long, cold steep doesn't just extract more slowly — it extracts *differently*. Certain acids and aromatic compounds are more soluble at higher temperatures than others, so a cold, slow extraction pulls a different ratio of compounds out of the same beans compared to a hot brew, even accounting for time. Cold brew consistently comes out lower in perceived acidity and higher in body and sweetness than the same beans brewed hot, which is why it's often described as "smoother" — it's not smoother because it's less strong (it's usually stronger, hence diluting it), it's smoother because the chemistry of what's dissolved is genuinely different.

## Why We Use a Concentrate, Not a Ready-to-Drink Ratio

Brewing at 1:8 and diluting to taste at serving time, rather than brewing straight to drinking strength, keeps the concentrate stable and useful for longer (it keeps about two weeks refrigerated), lets us serve it over ice without over-diluting from the ice melt, and gives us — and you — control over strength per drink rather than one fixed recipe for everyone.

We batch-brew this in a large cold brew tower behind the counter, started the evening before service, so it's ready fresh for us each morning rather than sitting around for days on end.
