This is the lot from the cooperative in Huehuetenango I visited back in October 2024 — it's taken until now to ship, rest, and get roasted, and it was worth the wait. This year they processed part of the harvest as red honey rather than fully washed, and we picked it specifically to run alongside our usual washed offering from the same farm.

**In the cup:** dried apricot, brown sugar, and a rounder, fuller body than the washed lot from this same cooperative, with noticeably less sharp acidity.

**Why it tastes like this:** in honey processing, the skin is removed but some of the sticky mucilage layer is left on the parchment during drying — "red honey" means a moderate amount of mucilage retained, more than yellow honey, less than black. That retained mucilage keeps fermenting gently during the drying period, adding body and a stone-fruit sweetness that a fully washed process (where the mucilage is stripped off before drying) doesn't develop. It's genuinely a middle ground between the clarity of washed coffee and the intensity of a full natural.

**Side by side with the washed lot:** if you want to actually taste what processing does independent of terroir, ask for the cupping flight this week — we're running this honey lot next to the cooperative's washed lot from the same farm, same harvest. Same trees, same altitude, same varietal, genuinely different cup.

**How we're brewing it:** batch brew, 1:17, 93°C. The fuller body carries well through batch brewing, and this is a good "house coffee" cup for anyone who finds our usual washed African lots too sharp.

On the bar now, while it lasts — this is a smaller lot than usual, since the cooperative only honey-processed a fraction of this harvest as an experiment.
