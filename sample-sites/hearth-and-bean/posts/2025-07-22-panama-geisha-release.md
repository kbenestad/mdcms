We don't run this coffee often, because we can't get much of it and it isn't cheap, but a small lot of washed Geisha from a high-altitude farm in the Panama highlands just arrived, and it's worth talking about even if most of it will sell out within the week.

**In the cup:** jasmine, bergamot, and a delicate, tea-like sweetness, with a lightness of body that reads almost more like a fine tea than a coffee. This is the coffee that made Geisha famous when a Panamanian lot took the coffee world by surprise at auction in 2004, and two decades on, well-grown Geisha still tastes like nothing else we serve.

**Why it's different:** Geisha (sometimes spelled Gesha) is a distinct varietal, originally from Ethiopia, that happened to thrive unusually well when grown at high altitude in a handful of Central American microclimates. It's a genuinely different genetic line from the Bourbon and Typica varietals underlying most of what we sell, and it produces a floral, tea-like cup profile almost regardless of how it's processed — though washed processing, like this lot, tends to show that floral character most clearly, with the least interference.

**Why it's expensive:** the plants are lower-yielding than more common varietals, the best lots come from a small number of specific microclimates, and demand from roasters chasing that jasmine-bergamot profile has pushed prices at auction into genuinely extraordinary territory for the very top lots. What we have isn't an auction-lot rarity, but it's still a small, carefully selected batch, and it's priced accordingly — $9 for a cup, $34 for a 100g bag (yes, 100g, not 250g).

**How we're brewing it:** V60, 1:17 — slightly more dilute than our usual filter ratio, since this coffee's delicate florals get lost if the cup is too concentrated. Water at 92°C, a touch cooler than usual, to keep the jasmine notes from tipping into anything sharp.

Limited quantity, first-come. Worth it, at least once.
