This week's filter bar coffee is a natural-processed lot from the Yirgacheffe growing region in southern Ethiopia, grown by smallholders at 1,900–2,100 metres and processed at a local washing station despite the name — "natural" here refers to the drying method, not the station.

**In the cup:** strawberry, jasmine, and a fermented, almost wine-like sweetness underneath. This is a much heavier-bodied, more intensely fruity cup than the washed Kenyan we ran in February, and it's a good side-by-side if you want to taste what processing method alone can do to the same broad region's coffee.

**Why it tastes like this:** in natural processing, the whole cherry is dried intact with the seed still inside the fruit, for two to four weeks depending on weather. The seed ferments surrounded by fruit sugars the entire time, which is where the strawberry and wine-like notes come from — they're fermentation byproducts that get absorbed into the bean, not something added afterward. It's a riskier method than washing (uneven drying can introduce off-flavours), which is part of why a clean natural lot commands a premium.

**How we're brewing it:** AeroPress, inverted, 1:15, 88°C, 2-minute steep. The heavier body and lower acidity of this lot holds up well to the fuller immersion brewing gives you, and the slightly lower temperature keeps the fermented notes from tipping into anything overripe.

By the cup or by the bag — ask for "the Ethiopian natural" at the counter.
