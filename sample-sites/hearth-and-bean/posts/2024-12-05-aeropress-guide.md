The AeroPress is the brew method we recommend most for travel and home use — cheap, close to indestructible, and forgiving of a slightly wrong grind in a way a V60 isn't. Here's our house recipe.

## The Recipe

- **Coffee:** 16g, medium grind (slightly coarser than table salt)
- **Water:** 240g, 88°C
- **Ratio:** 1:15
- **Total time:** ~2:00

## Method

1. **Invert the AeroPress** — plunger inserted a short way into the chamber, resting upside down on the wide end. This lets you do a full immersion steep before pressing, rather than water dripping through immediately like the standard method.
2. **Add coffee, then water**, all 240g at once, and stir gently for 10 seconds to make sure all the grounds are saturated.
3. **Steep for 1:30.** This is a full immersion brew for most of that time, which is why the grind can be a bit coarser and more forgiving than a pour-over — the coffee has much longer contact with the water regardless of flow rate.
4. **Attach the rinsed filter and cap**, carefully flip the whole assembly onto your cup, and press slowly and steadily over about 20–30 seconds. You want gentle, even resistance — if it's very hard to press, your grind is too fine; if it slides through with no resistance at all, too coarse.

## Why the Inverted Method

Standard (right-side-up) AeroPress use starts extracting the moment you add water, since it's already sitting on the filter and gravity pulls water through immediately — hard to control precisely, and prone to dripping through before you're ready to press. Inverting turns the AeroPress into a sealed immersion brewer for the steep phase, giving you full control over contact time independent of press speed. It's a small change that makes the method noticeably more consistent.

Lower water temperature than a V60 (88°C vs. 94°C) balances out the longer contact time — full immersion for 90 seconds at 94°C would push a lot of coffees into over-extraction. If you're brewing a very light roast and want more clarity, bump the temperature up to 90–92°C and shorten the steep slightly.

We keep three AeroPresses behind the counter for our own use on slow mornings — it's genuinely a great everyday brewer, not just a travel backup.
