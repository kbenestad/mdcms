The V60 is the brew method we use most at the bar, and the one we get asked to explain most often. Here's our house recipe, plus the reasoning behind each step so you can adjust it instead of just copying it.

## The Recipe

- **Coffee:** 20g, medium-fine grind (think granulated sugar)
- **Water:** 320g, 94°C (about 30 seconds off a rolling boil at sea level)
- **Ratio:** 1:16
- **Total time:** 2:30–3:00

## Method

1. **Rinse the filter** with hot water, then discard the rinse water. This removes papery taste from the filter and preheats the dripper so it doesn't pull heat out of your brew.
2. **Add coffee, level the bed, and start your timer as you begin pouring.**
3. **Bloom:** pour 40g of water (2x the coffee weight) in a slow spiral, just enough to saturate all the grounds. Wait 30–45 seconds. This is the CO₂ escaping — you'll see it bubble and rise. Skipping the bloom means that gas escapes unevenly during the main pour instead, which pushes water away from parts of the coffee bed and causes uneven extraction.
4. **Main pour:** pour the remaining 280g in slow concentric circles, working from the centre outward and back, avoiding the very edge of the filter. Aim to finish adding water by around 1:45–2:00.
5. **Let it drain fully.** Total time from first pour to the last drip should land around 2:30–3:00. Faster than that and you're likely under-extracted (thin, sour); much slower and you're probably over-extracted (bitter, muddy) or your grind is too fine.

## Why These Numbers

A 1:16 ratio is a reasonable middle ground for filter coffee — strong enough to taste like something, dilute enough that a slight timing error doesn't ruin the cup. If you like a bolder cup, go to 1:15; for something lighter, 1:17. The bloom water at 2x coffee weight is enough to fully saturate the grounds without starting real extraction before the gas has a chance to escape.

Grind size is the lever to reach for if your timing is off: too fast (under 2:15) and the cup tastes sour or weak — grind finer. Too slow (over 3:15) and it tastes bitter or muddy — grind coarser. Water temperature matters too, but grind size is the bigger, easier-to-control variable, so change one thing at a time.
