I spent the first week of October in Huehuetenango, in the western highlands of Guatemala, visiting the cooperative we've bought from for three harvests running. This is the fourth sourcing trip we've done since opening, and it's still the part of the job that reminds me why any of this is worth doing carefully.

The farms here sit between 1,500 and 2,000 metres, which is high enough that frost is an occasional real risk — something I hadn't fully appreciated until a farmer walked me through a stand of trees still showing damage from a cold snap two years back. High elevation is a big part of why this region's coffee has the density and structure it does: the plants grow more slowly at altitude, and slower growth means more time for sugars to develop in the cherry. But it's also a genuine agricultural risk that the farmers manage constantly, not just a marketing detail on a bag.

I spent a morning on the wet mill watching this year's harvest get depulped and moved into fermentation tanks, timed by taste and touch rather than a clock — the person running the mill has done this for over twenty years and can tell the mucilage is ready to wash off by feel, which is a skill I have enormous respect for and no ability to replicate myself. We cupped six lots together, in the same room, using the same protocol we use back home, and picked the two we're bringing back this year based on that session rather than on samples that arrived by mail months ago.

We pay above the cooperative's standard export price, on a contract we agree before the harvest so the farmers know what they're growing for rather than finding out at sale time. It's a small operation on our end — we're buying maybe 2,000kg a year from this cooperative, which is nothing compared to what larger roasters move — but the relationship is real, and I'd rather have four farms I actually visit than forty I only know from a spec sheet.

New lot from this trip lands on the bar in a few weeks, once it's shipped, rested, and roasted. I'll post tasting notes when it's ready.
